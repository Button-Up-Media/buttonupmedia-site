The problem

Common reasons restaurant social media doesn’t work

These are the approaches restaurants usually try first, and why they tend to fall short.

  1. 01

    Doing it yourself

    You’re already handling food costs, staffing and vendors. When social media gets whatever time is left, posts go out when someone remembers, photos get taken on a phone during service, and there’s no plan behind any of it.

  2. 02

    Hiring a general marketing agency

    You hire an agency and get stock photos, Canva graphics and captions that could be for any business, because nobody from the agency has been inside your restaurant.

  3. 03

    Paying influencers

    You pay an influencer to come in and they post a story or a Reel. You might get a busy week and some new followers, but when their audience moves on, so does the traffic. More on this below.

  4. 04

    What’s usually missing

    What usually helps is a partner who knows restaurants, makes the content at your place and posts on a regular schedule. That’s how social media becomes a reliable way to bring in customers.

A closer look

Paying influencers vs. building your own following

Short term

The influencer cycle

You pay for a post, get a short bump, and then have to pay again to get it back.

  1. You pay an influencer for a post
  2. You get a busy week
  3. Their followers move on
  4. Bookings go back to normal
Then you pay again
Long term

Building your own following

It takes longer to build, but the followers stay yours.

  1. Post your own content on a regular schedule
  2. Build a following that wants to hear from you
  3. Reach those people any time you want to fill tables

The goal is a following that belongs to your restaurant, so you can reach it whenever you need to.

Read the full explanation

When bookings slow down, a lot of restaurants do the same thing: they pay an influencer to come in. The influencer posts a story or a Reel, and for a week or so the room is busier. Then the influencer moves on to the next spot, and so does their audience.

The people who follow food influencers are looking for the next place to try. Even if they loved your food, if your own page doesn’t give them a reason to follow you, they won’t, and they’ll forget about you. The next time they go out, they’re back on that influencer’s feed, and the only way to reach them again is to pay for another post.

With your own account posting consistently, you build a following of people who want to hear from you. When business is slow, you can post a story or run a promotion and reach them directly. Paid ads and influencers still help, but they work best on top of an account people already follow.

The framework

How to promote a restaurant on social media

Before anything gets posted, get clear on these four things. They decide what kind of content and ads make sense for your restaurant.

  1. 01

    Know what makes you different

    Be clear on why someone should pick your restaurant over the one down the street. Once that’s clear, the content and captions are much easier to get right.

  2. 02

    Decide on your goals

    Decide what growth means for you. Some restaurants want to grow their audience as fast as possible, and others want to grow more carefully and protect a strict brand image. Each needs a different plan.

  3. 03

    Set a budget and targets

    Set a realistic budget and revenue goal, plus softer goals like more followers, more trust or more repeat visits. Clear targets make it easier to decide what to do first.

  4. 04

    Make a plan and stick to it

    Build a plan that fits your budget, your goals and how much you want to experiment. That might be organic content, paid social ads or both. Then post consistently and adjust based on what’s working.

FAQ

Restaurant social media questions

How our social media service works, plus general advice on posting, platforms and content.

01Do you film at my restaurant?
Yes. We film at your restaurant, wherever it is in the US, shooting your food, drinks, kitchen, dining room and staff. We plan the shoot with you ahead of time.
02Do you work with restaurants outside Miami?
Yes. We work with restaurants anywhere in the US, with the same service and the same quality of work. We’re headquartered in Miami, FL.
03How much content does one shoot produce?
One shoot typically produces 4 to 6 weeks of content. That footage becomes the Reels, TikToks and photo posts in your monthly content calendar.
04Do you post and reply to comments for us?
Yes. We write the captions, post everything on your monthly content calendar and answer comments and DMs every day, including reservation questions. You approve the calendar before anything goes out.
05How much does restaurant social media management cost?
It depends on how much content and management you need, and we set pricing on the free strategy call. Most restaurant owners invest $1,500 to $5,000 a month across a coordinated marketing program. Our social media service includes the shoots as well as the posting.
06Do you require a long-term contract?
No. We don’t require long-term contracts. We’ll go over pricing and what’s included on your free strategy call.
07Do you work in Spanish?
Yes. We work in English and Spanish, so we can write your captions and answer comments and DMs in either language, or both.
08How often should a restaurant post on social media?
Post on a schedule you can keep up every week. A few good posts every week will do more than a burst of posts followed by weeks of nothing. We set your posting schedule in your monthly content calendar, based on your goals and how much content your shoots produce.
09What social media platforms are best for restaurants?
For most restaurants, Instagram and TikTok matter most, and Facebook is most useful for ads. Instagram is where people look at your food and your space before they decide to visit, and TikTok is where a lot of people find new places to eat. Facebook ads are a good way to reach people near your restaurant.
10What type of content works best for restaurant social media?
Short videos usually work best: Reels, TikToks and YouTube Shorts of food being made, close-ups of dishes, cocktails being poured and the kitchen during service. Good food photos and photo carousels help too. Whatever you post should look like your restaurant, which stock photos can’t do.
11Should restaurants use TikTok?
Yes, most restaurants should. A lot of people look for places to eat on TikTok and Instagram, and the videos that do well there are usually simple: food being made, behind-the-scenes clips and a look at the kitchen during service.
12Can you handle social media for multiple restaurant locations?
Yes. We work with independent restaurants and multi-location groups. Groups get one content strategy so the brand stays consistent, plus content and promotions for each location.
13What is the difference between organic and paid social media?
Organic social is what you post on your own accounts, and paid social is ads on Instagram and Facebook shown to the people you choose, like people near your restaurant. Organic builds a following that wants to hear from you, and paid reaches people who haven’t found you yet. We can plan both together.
14How is Button Up Media different from other social media agencies?
We make the content at your restaurant instead of only writing captions and scheduling posts. We set up shoots at your place to film your food, your dining room and your team, and build your accounts around that footage. We also only work with restaurants, bars and cafes.
