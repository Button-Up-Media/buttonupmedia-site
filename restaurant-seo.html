The basics

What restaurant SEO actually means.

Restaurant SEO (search engine optimization) is the work that gets your restaurant shown when someone nearby searches for a place to eat. Right now, someone a few blocks away is typing “restaurants near me”. They're hungry and ready to book, and Google is choosing who they see first. For a restaurant, that choice happens in three places.

Google Maps and the map pack

The map pack is the box of three restaurants Google shows above the regular results for searches like “sushi near me”. Your Google Business Profile decides whether you're in it.

The regular Google results

Your website, your menu page and your listings on Yelp, TripAdvisor and OpenTable, ranking for your cuisine, your neighborhood and the occasions you're built for.

AI answers

ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Gemini and Perplexity now answer “where should I eat tonight?” directly. Answer engine optimization (AEO) makes sure they have the right facts about you.

How a guest picks a restaurant

  1. Pulls out their phone
  2. Searches “restaurants near me”
  3. Scans the top map results
  4. Checks stars, photos and the menu
  5. Taps call, directions or reserve

The whole decision happens on one screen, often before anyone opens your website. Restaurant SEO is how you become one of the options on that screen.

Miami is its own search market

Tourists, locals and two languages, all searching at once.

A visitor in a South Beach hotel types “restaurants near me” and picks from the map. A local searches by neighborhood and craving. A family in Doral searches in Spanish. Good local SEO covers all three: the map pack for near me searches, cuisine and neighborhood keywords for locals, and Spanish pages and replies for guests who search in Spanish. It also plans around the calendar, from snowbird season to the big event weekends.

The problem

Why your restaurant isn't showing up on Google.

Your food can be incredible and your service flawless. If Google can't clearly understand your restaurant, it shows someone else. You opened a restaurant to cook, not to learn search engines, so these gaps are normal. Here's what we find most often when we audit a restaurant.

A Google profile set up once, then forgotten

The wrong primary category, old hours, no menu or reservation link, the same photos from opening week and reviews nobody answered.

What it costs you: Google's own guidance says complete, accurate profiles are easier to match to searches. A stale one slides down the map.

A menu that only lives in a PDF or a photo

It's slow to open on a phone, and search engines and AI tools read plain text on a web page far more reliably than a scanned menu.

What it costs you: your signature dishes never show up for the searches that matter, like “birria tacos” or “omakase Miami Beach”.

A website that says “Welcome” instead of what you are

It was built to look good, not to rank. No cuisine or neighborhood in the page titles, and no schema markup (the code that tells Google “this is a restaurant, here are the hours”).

What it costs you: if your site never says “Cuban restaurant in Little Havana”, Google won't guess it.

Listings that disagree with each other

An old phone number on Yelp, last year's hours on Apple Maps, a different name on a delivery app, a closed location that still shows up.

What it costs you: mixed signals make Google, and the AI tools that read those sites, less sure which version is true.

No routine for reviews

Waiting for reviews to happen on their own, and leaving the unhappy ones unanswered for the next guest to read.

What it costs you: Google says review count and rating factor into local ranking, and guests read reviews right before they choose.

English only, in a bilingual city

No Spanish page, no Spanish description of what you serve, no replies in Spanish to Spanish reviews.

What it costs you: guests searching in Spanish find the restaurant down the street that speaks to them.

Google doesn't reward the best restaurant. It rewards the one it understands best.

What we do

What our restaurant SEO service includes.

Six pieces, run together, so Google, Maps and AI tools all tell the same story about your restaurant. This is built for how diners actually search for a place to eat, not a generic playbook borrowed from law firms and dentists.

01

Google Business Profile optimization

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that shows on Maps and in the map pack. For most restaurants it's the biggest single lever, so we start here.

  • The right primary and secondary categories
  • Menu, reservation and online ordering links
  • Attributes like outdoor seating, happy hour and reservations
  • Weekly posts for specials, events and holidays
  • Fresh photos and short videos, uploaded regularly

If we also run your social media and content, the photos and videos from our on-site shoots feed your profile too.

02

Local keywords and on-page SEO

We find the searches you can actually win, in English and Spanish, then build them into your website.

  • Cuisine and neighborhood: “ramen Downtown Miami”
  • Occasions: “date night Coral Gables”, “birthday dinner Brickell”
  • Dishes and diets: “gluten-free pizza”, “vegan Cuban food”
  • Page titles, headings and a real HTML menu page
  • Pages for private events, catering, brunch and happy hour
03

Technical SEO

The behind-the-scenes fixes that let Google read your site and let guests use it on a phone.

  • Mobile speed and image compression
  • Restaurant, menu and FAQ schema markup
  • Broken links, redirects and crawl errors
  • Google Search Console and call and booking tracking

If your platform blocks the basics, we'll tell you straight. Our restaurant website design builds ship SEO-ready from day one.

04

Citations and listings

A citation is any listing of your name, address and phone number. We make them match everywhere guests and AI tools look.

  • Apple Maps (Apple Business Connect) and Bing Places
  • Yelp, TripAdvisor and Facebook
  • OpenTable, Resy and the delivery apps
  • Duplicate and closed listings cleaned up
05

Reviews and reputation

A simple routine that brings in steady, honest reviews and answers every one, so the next guest sees a restaurant that listens.

  • Easy ways for happy guests to leave a review
  • Replies to every review, in English or Spanish
  • A plan for handling negative reviews
  • Review monitoring and competitor benchmarking

We never buy reviews or only ask the guests we know are happy. Google's review policies ban both.

06

AI answers (AEO)

We give ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Gemini and Perplexity clear, consistent facts, so they describe you correctly when someone asks where to eat.

  • The same name, hours and details everywhere
  • Clear answers on your site: parking, dress code, kids, private rooms
  • Structured data machines can read
  • Mentions in local press and “best of” lists where we can earn them

Nobody can promise an AI tool will recommend you. We make you the easiest restaurant to recommend accurately.

FAQ

Restaurant SEO questions, answered.

01

What is restaurant SEO?

Restaurant SEO is the work that gets your restaurant shown when people nearby search for a place to eat on Google, Google Maps and AI tools like ChatGPT. It covers your Google Business Profile, your website, your listings on sites like Yelp and TripAdvisor, and your reviews.

02

Why does my Google Business Profile matter so much?

It's the listing that shows up on Google Maps and in the map pack, the three restaurants Google shows above the regular results for searches like “restaurants near me”. Guests check your hours, photos, stars and menu there before they ever open your website, so for most restaurants it's the biggest single lever in local search.

03

How long does restaurant SEO take to work?

Google Business Profile and listing fixes usually move first. Website rankings take longer and build month over month, so think in quarters, not weeks. On the strategy call we set expectations for your specific neighborhood and competition.

04

How much does restaurant SEO cost?

Pricing is set on the strategy call, based on your market, your number of locations and what's already in place. Most restaurants invest $1,500 to $5,000 a month across a coordinated marketing program, and there are no long-term contracts.

05

Do you do SEO in Spanish?

Yes. Our team is bilingual, so we research Spanish searches, write Spanish pages and reply to Spanish reviews in Spanish. In Miami a lot of guests search in Spanish, and those searches are easy to miss when your website only speaks English.

06

Can you get my restaurant recommended by ChatGPT or Google's AI?

No one can guarantee it, and you should be wary of anyone who does. What we can do is make your facts match everywhere AI tools look, answer common guest questions clearly on your website, add structured data and earn local mentions. That makes your restaurant easier to recommend, and to describe correctly.

07

Do I need a new website for SEO?

Not always. We start with the site you have. If your platform blocks the basics, like editable page titles, a real menu page or decent speed on a phone, we'll tell you, because a rebuild can cost less than fighting a bad platform for a year.

08

Do you work with restaurant groups and multiple locations?

Yes. Each location gets its own optimized Google Business Profile, its own page on your website, its own listings cleanup and its own line in the monthly report, so you can see how every room performs.

09

Do I keep everything if I stop working with you?

Yes. Your Google Business Profile, your website changes, your listings and your reports stay yours. We don't hold anything hostage.
